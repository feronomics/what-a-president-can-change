# Increases and reductions in original program spending

Compare the year before a government enters with its second year, using the same horizon for every administration. No inflation adjustment. A program's monetary change is final execution minus baseline execution.

The overall figure shows two separately calculated amounts for each president: sum of monetary increases and sum of monetary reductions. Divide both by total baseline-year execution, a fixed denominator that does not change with the final budget. This makes differently sized governments comparable. The numerator is an actual difference in original amounts, not a change in program budget shares.

The summary does not assume increases are financed by reductions. Spending can grow: gross increases plus signed reductions must equal final total minus baseline total. This identity was checked for all five governments. Increasing and declining execution are descriptive observations, not documented transfer orders or independently identified presidential choices.

One additional figure per president identifies the five largest monetary increases and five largest monetary reductions. Each row shows its amount in the baseline and comparison year; bars show the difference, all in millions of quetzales. Use the summary for cross-government size comparisons and the individual figures for program detail.

1_resumen.csv contains aggregate amounts, initial denominator and normalized percentages. 9_montos.csv contains all programs. 10_destacados.csv contains displayed selections. All 16 supplied groups are included; program identities are ministry plus Programa_Final, with no unverified recoding of policy continuity. Supporting program-share data are retained separately, but are not the public figure's measure.
